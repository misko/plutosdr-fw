# Issue 123: positive PLL lock incorrectly reported as a timeout

The failure was reproduced on `192.168.1.20`, serial
`1040005e0b100007100010000bf33a5d4d`, with a diagnostic-only image that retained
the v0.60 timeout rule. The failing PLL register read returned `VCO_LOCK=2`,
but the driver checked its two-millisecond deadline before interpreting that
positive status. A delayed read therefore became `-ETIMEDOUT` even though the
radio had reported lock. The fixed driver accepts that positive observation;
unlocked polls still time out and SPI errors still fail immediately.

## Evidence and scope

The matched production runner used dual RX, native protocol v3 at 10 MS/s,
10 MHz analog bandwidth, manual gain 40 dB, one-million-sample transport
blocks, sixteen kernel buffers, 120 ms dwells, 20 ms transition budget,
feedback every eight visits and its normal NVMe IQ archive workload. The upper
frequency set was 1190/1440/1690/1940 MHz. The diagnostic run failed after
about 72.5 seconds, with 531 planned, 529 delivered and two cancelled visits.
Terminal reason 3 carried `-110`; restoration succeeded.

The retained first failure identifies session `15525937930749536918`, visit
530, profile 2, recall stage and PLL lock step. One SPI poll returned status
2. Its elapsed time was **5,212,626 ns**, with **5,211,432 ns** in the measured
SPI call interval. The source counter advanced by 52,142 samples, consistent
with that interval at 10 MS/s. The DMA refill maximum was about 134 ms, below
the five-second watchdog. A matched five-minute 2.5 MS/s upper-edge control
completed on the diagnostic image.

The SPI interval includes scheduling and service delay; it does not prove the
bus itself took five milliseconds. This establishes the false-timeout
mechanism on the affected radio. It does not establish that every historical
failure had this cause, or quantify the cause of the scheduling delay. No
independent physical RF frequency measurement was performed on each visit.

## Change

The driver checks negative SPI status first, positive lock second, then the
deadline for an unlocked poll. The recall counter is sampled after all
attestation; userspace continues to exclude the measured transition and its
settling guard. Accepting a late positive poll does not backdate a receipt or
admit IQ from before lock. Existing selector, SRAM, profile CRC and frequency
checks remain in the recall path.

Additive diagnostics use a versioned, bounded 32-event kernel ring with
session/visit/profile, operation stage, monotonic timestamps, source counters,
poll count, raw lock status and SPI timing. The first failure and restoration
failure have separate retained records even after ring rollover. The kernel
snapshot survives release until the next acquire. No per-hop printing,
persistent write or allocation was added to the polling path.

iiOD exposes `SCANDIAG <device> 16`, followed by little-endian session and
generation u64 values. The response is a decimal length and at most 32 KiB of
`spf.scan-diagnostics/v1` JSON. It rejects stale session identities, works
before CLOSE after terminal restoration, and reports unsupported diagnostics
on older kernels. The terminal's original errno remains distinct from the
restoration errno. Recall, DMA watchdog, producer and session errors carry
separate stages.

PPU collects the snapshot before teardown and offers a failure receipt sink.
The Leo runner atomically saves that small receipt before archive abort, so
discarding partial IQ no longer discards the error evidence. Receipt or
archive cleanup errors add exception notes while preserving the first error.
The deployed capture rate policy, RX mask, gain and cadence are retained.

## Red/green and integration validation

The regression compiles the production lock helper with controlled SPI and
clock behavior. A locked read completing at 2.5 ms fails on the original and
diagnostic-only code, then passes on the fix. Delayed clear status remains
`-ETIMEDOUT`, negative SPI status remains `-EIO`, and continuous clear polling
remains bounded. The complete production-function fault suite passes 48
scenarios, including late-lock receipt timing, restoration failure and ring
rollover. ARM kernel and iiOD builds succeed.

Six native daemon suites pass, together with ASan/UBSan session validation.
Forty PPU client/campaign tests and twenty Leo runner tests cover response
bounds, stale identities, backward compatibility, persistence ordering and
cleanup error preservation. The remote immutable source graph passes.

The exact fixed image passed a RAM-only canary capture at 10 MS/s (73/73
delivered), intentional client cancellation after three visits and an injected
DMA failure at 2.5 MS/s. Each restored receiver settings and allowed a
32,768-byte ordinary dual-RX capture. The DMA failure retained stage 5 and
`-EIO` separately from successful restoration. The canary was rebooted back
to its unchanged persistent v0.59 image afterward.

Repeated five-minute production qualification results are recorded in the
accompanying qualification JSON before release promotion.
